P0009 Discarded DATA padding is omitted from flow control


P0010    [src/proto/streams/recv.rs:580](/work/crate/src/proto/streams/recv.rs:580).


P0011    Expected: DATA flow control accounts for the entire frame payload, including padding and its length byte. Discarded padding capacity must become reusable.


P0012    Observed: accounting uses only the body length after padding has been stripped.


P0013    Consequence: a conforming peer can exhaust its window and stall, even after the application consumes and releases all body data.


P0014    Evidence: executed `padded_data_returns_discarded_padding_capacity`. A 16-byte stream window was filled with one body byte, one padding-length byte, and fourteen padding bytes. After releasing the body byte, no WINDOW_UPDATE arrived within 100 ms. The equivalent unpadded control passed. Source inspection confirms padding never reaches the receive accounting.


P0015    account for all flow-controlled octets and automatically release discarded padding at both stream and connection levels.


P0016 Reading an empty body consumes and loses its trailing headers. src/proto/streams/recv.rs:1116. When a response consists of initial HEADERS followed directly by trailing HEADERS with END_STREAM, RecvStream::data() pops the next receive event while looking for DATA. Its non-DATA branch returns None and consumes the trailer event. Consequently a normal caller that finishes the body loop before calling trailers() gets Ok(None), losing application metadata such as x-result: ok on responses that contain no DATA frames.
